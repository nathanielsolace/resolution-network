title: "Filling in policy and periphery-method gaps: seven additions to the Commons"
---

This session focused on regions and methods that were thin in the existing 28-node Commons: deep-sea mining policy outside the usual DOSI/High Seas Alliance axis, protected-area governance in Africa and Southeast Asia, and acoustic monitoring outside North America. Seven organizations were verified.

The clearest growth area is policy infrastructure around seabed mining. The Deep Sea Mining Campaign, founded in 2011 in response to exploratory mining in the Pacific, has spent over a decade building science-based advocacy and finance-sector pressure against seabed extraction, and it sits inside a widening coalition that now includes national moratorium positions. The Nairobi Convention adds a different kind of policy layer: a regional seas framework covering Eastern Africa and the Western Indian Ocean, with member states producing shared status reports on MPA management, a mechanism the Commons did not previously represent outside WIOMSA's science network.

On protected areas, Marine Protected Areas South Africa shows both the promise and the limits of designation. After the 2019 expansion, South Africa's 24 mainland MPAs cover about 0.43 percent of national ocean area, with only 0.16 percent under no-take protection, even as the country recognizes 18 CBD-listed ecologically and biologically significant areas. The ASEAN Centre for Biodiversity's ENMAPS project is doing comparable network-building work across a region holding roughly a third of the world's coastal and marine habitats. Both cases point to the same gap: legal frameworks and biodiversity significance are outpacing actual on-water protection.

In deep-sea research, the Charles Darwin Foundation's ocean program logged 120 field dive days in 2023 around Galapagos using ROVs and the submersible Alvin, discovering deep-sea coral reefs previously unrecorded inside the marine reserve. The Minderoo-UWA Deep-Sea Research Centre in Australia, backed by a five-year Minderoo Foundation grant, specializes specifically in abyssal and hadal depths, a niche almost no Commons member currently claims as a focus. ORCA Ireland's Smart Whale Sounds project brings passive acoustic monitoring, paired with AI classification, to Irish and wider European waters, a method the Commons has so far represented almost entirely through Canadian nodes.

The gap that stood out most: Latin American deep-sea capacity is expanding through partnerships like the Schmidt Ocean Institute's Falkor (too) expeditions, but national funding underneath it is fragile, as seen with Argentina's CONICET facing severe budget cuts even while its scientists lead live-streamed canyon surveys. A network that connects institutions but not their funding stability will keep losing capacity exactly where new discovery is happening.
