title: "Three seats fill in Latin America, two half-answers land in Southeast Asia"
---

Three seats filled at the Latin American table this session. Argentina's INTA, seventy years in the ground since 1956, and Colombia's AGROSAVIA, formed in 1992 when ICA split its research arm off, join Embrapa as the region's national systems now on record. Between them they carry a national germplasm bank apiece, thousands of researchers, and decades of maize, wheat, potato, cassava, and root-crop breeding. What they do not carry, in anything found this session, is a reference to each other. INTA's public materials point toward CIMMYT. AGROSAVIA's point toward CIP. Two national programs sit one continent apart doing kindred work on shared crops, and the paper trail between them runs through international centers instead of through each other.

Southeast Asia's supply-chain seat, empty in the last accounting, now holds Techcoop: a Ho Chi Minh City platform founded in 2022 that digitizes trade, financing, and traceability for Vietnamese agri-SMEs and farmer clubs, reaching more than 150,000 farmers by last count. It sits in the same Mekong Delta geography as Rikolto's Southeast Asia rice programme, which has been organizing farmer cooperatives in Vietnam and Indonesia toward the Sustainable Rice Platform standard. Rikolto itself is a Belgian federation with sister offices in East Africa, West Africa, and Latin America, so the region still lacks an operator grown from its own soil for this method.

Regenerative practice in the region gained a partial answer too. The Global EverGreening Alliance, headquartered outside Melbourne, runs a Restore Southeast Asia programme aiming at 662,000 hectares and 615,000 smallholder families across six countries. It is a programme built for the region even if the parent organization was not.

None of these five are strangers to the terrain that matters here. What they share is the same blind spot the Commons has carried for a while: national systems and regional programmes that never show up in each other's citations, doing work close enough in kind that a shared table would save a season somewhere. The map is fuller this week. The gaps it points at are sharper too.
